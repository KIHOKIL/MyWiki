Title: Korea’s Nominal GDP Growth Hits 47-Year High

Published: Tue, 08 Sep 2026 06:59:00 GMT

South Korea’s economy maintained solid growth in the second quarter, supported by strong exports led by semiconductors and a recovery in private consumption. Nominal gross domestic product (GDP) growth hit its highest level in 47 years, while real gross national income (GNI) improved sharply, fueling expectations that South Korea could reach the $40,000 per capita GNI milestone in U.S. dollar terms. According to the Bank of Korea’s preliminary Second Quarter 2026 National Income data released Sept. 8, real GDP grew 0.6% from the previous quarter. The figure was unchanged from the advance estimate released in July and followed a sharp rebound of 1.8% in the first quarter after the economy contracted 0.1% in the fourth quarter of last year. From a year earlier, the economy grew 3.7%, marking a more stable growth trajectory. By expenditure, exports rose 1.3%, driven by semiconductors, machinery and equipment, leading overall growth. Imports also increased 0.7%, mainly on automobiles and machinery, but the increase in exports outpaced imports, with net exports contributing 0.3 percentage point to overall growth. In domestic demand, private consumption rose 0.4%, supported by increased spending on goods such as home appliances and services including food and accommodation. Investment in intellectual property products also increased 3.4%, led by research and development and software.
