Title: Kyobo Securities - Fed May Consider 'Hawkish Rate Hike' if Geopolitical Risks Persist

U.S. Federal Reserve (Fed)Yonhap News file photo (Seoul=Yonhap Infomax) Hak Seong Kim - The U.S. Federal Reserve could discuss a "hawkish rate hike" at its September Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting as geopolitical risks intensify, according to a new analysis. "If geopolitical risks do not subside, the Fed may discuss a hawkish rate hike rather than the hawkish hold or dovish hike that markets are expecting," said Wi Jae-hyun, a researcher at Kyobo Securities, on the 11th. He noted that with West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude futures surpassing $100 per barrel again, the September Consumer Price Index (CPI) is likely to rebound, which could lead markets to discount the significance of the August CPI data. Regarding the U.S. August Producer Price Index (PPI) released overnight, Wi assessed that there were many concerning elements. The August PPI rose 5.4% year-over-year and 0.4% month-over-month. The annual figure slightly exceeded expectations of 5.3%. Core PPI increased 4.6% year-over-year, meeting expectations. "Goods prices rose 1.1% month-over-month, driving headline inflation higher," he said. "Within goods prices, diesel fuel surged 24% month-over-month, accounting for three-quarters of the monthly price increase." He also pointed out that transportation services and airfares rose 4.1% and 4.2% month-over-month respectively, adding that "concerns about secondary spillover effects are expanding as rising energy prices impact transportation costs."
