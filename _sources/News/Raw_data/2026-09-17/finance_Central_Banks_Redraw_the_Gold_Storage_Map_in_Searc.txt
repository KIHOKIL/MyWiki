Title: Central Banks Redraw the Gold Storage Map in Search of Security and Liquidity

Published: Tue, 15 Sep 2026 09:11:00 GMT

Central banks are increasingly redistributing their gold reserves between domestic vaults and major global trading hubs, marking a shift beyond the earlier wave of gold repatriation and reflecting growing efforts to balance security, accessibility and liquidity during crises.A recent World Gold Council report found that cross-border gold movements are no longer driven solely by the desire to strengthen national control over reserves. Instead, they have become part of a more active approach to managing storage locations, ensuring that gold can be traded or mobilised quickly when needed.The Netherlands’ latest move illustrates this trend. On 2 September, De Nederlandsche Bank announced that it had transferred and reallocated around 86 tonnes of gold from New York and Ottawa to London in an operation conducted between March and August 2026.The operation was intended to improve the liquidity and tradability of the Dutch gold reserve while strengthening the central bank’s preparedness for severe crises, including circumstances that might require gold to be used in international settlements or to provide emergency liquidity.London Becomes the Largest Storage Location for Dutch GoldThe operation increased London’s share of Dutch gold reserves from 18.1% to 32.1%, making the British capital the largest single storage location for the Netherlands’ gold, ahead of the 30.8% held domestically.Meanwhile, the share stored in New York fell from 31.3% to 18.5%, while Ottawa’s share declined
