Title: Hybrid solar forecasting combining physical and machine learning models boosts accuracy for grid operators and traders

Published: Tue, 08 Sep 2026 17:01:00 GMT

Solar power forecasting is moving from academic experiments to core grid and market tools across Africa and beyond. A new study titled ‘Hybrid photovoltaic power forecasting by combining physical and machine learning models,’shows that hybrid models which blend physical solar conversion equations with modern machine learning deliver the strongest day ahead forecasts for photovoltaic plants. The work tested systems in Utrecht in the Netherlands and Fortaleza in Brazil, covering different climates and installation types, and found consistent benefits from the hybrid approach. The headline result is a meaningful drop in forecast error. Compared with the best pure physical model chain, hybrid models reduced mean absolute error by up to 21% in Utrecht and up to 35% in Fortaleza. Against pure machine learning baselines, the hybrid setups cut errors by up to 24% and 43% respectively. These gains matter for dispatch planning, reserve setting, and trading decisions where even small percentage improvements translate into lower balancing costs and fewer penalties. What drives the improvement is not the complexity of the physics layer but the choice of machine learning model. The study tested a range from simple linear regression to non-linear ensemble methods. Switching from linear models to Random Forest lowered average error by about 13% in Fortaleza and 15% in Utrecht for deterministic forecasts. For probabilistic forecasts, which provide prediction intervals rather than single point values, moving from quantile regression to quantile Random Forest cut the continuous ranked probability score by roughly 13% in Fortaleza and 17% in Utrecht. This pattern held across clear, partly cloudy and overcast sky conditions. By contrast, making the physical model more detailed or changing the hybridization method had minimal impact.
