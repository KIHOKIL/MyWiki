Palantir Technologieshas picked a new partner to power its push intosovereign AI infrastructure, and the choice says a lot about where theenterprise AI marketis heading. The company has namedNebius Groupas its preferred provider for sovereign AI infrastructure, a deal that folds Nebius’s purpose-built compute and inference technology directly inside Palantir’s security perimeter. For commercial customers, that means running AI workloads without ever handing control of their data, models, or compute to a general-purpose cloud provider. Summary The partnership positions Nebius as the go-to infrastructure supplier for organizations that wantsovereign AI infrastructurewithout building it themselves. Both companies trade on the Nasdaq, Palantir underPLTRand Nebius underNBIS, and the arrangement effectively merges Nebius’s hardware and compute expertise with Palantir’s software and security layer. Nebius’s AI compute and inference capabilities now sit inside Palantir’s security perimeter, rather than existing as a separate, outsourced service. That integration is the technical backbone of the deal. It means workloads can run on infrastructure designed specifically for AI from the start, something Palantir has flagged as a real point of difference from the generic cloud platforms most companies default to. Eligible commercial customers can now manage their own compute, data, and models without giving that control to a broader, general-purpose cloud vendor. This is the core selling point of the arrangement: enterprises get infrastructure built for AI, wrapped in the kind of oversight typically reserved for government-grade deployments, but without needing to be a government agency to access it. Beyond the security angle, the deal is built around flexibility, letting customers pair open AI models with their own proprietary information while shortening the time it takes to stand up new compute capacity.
