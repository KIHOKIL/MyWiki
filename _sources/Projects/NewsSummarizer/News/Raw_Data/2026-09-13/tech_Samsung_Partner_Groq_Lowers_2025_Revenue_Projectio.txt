Title: Samsung Partner Groq Lowers 2025 Revenue Projection by $1.5 Billion

Published: Wed, 30 Jul 2025 08:04:00 GMT

Groq, an artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor startup where Samsung Electronics has invested, has significantly lowered its revenue forecast for this year. Groq is also a partner of Samsung Electronics’ foundry business. This development presents an unexpected obstacle for Samsung Electronics’ foundry, which had recently found relief through securing Tesla’s "AI6" chipset order. On July 29 (local time), The Information reported that Groq has reduced its revenue target for this year from $2 billion to $500 million, a decrease of $1.5 billion. The revenue target reduction was confirmed during Groq’s recent process of raising an additional $600 million in investment. A Groq spokesperson explained, “Some revenue projections have been deferred to 2026 due to a lack of data center capacity in regions where semiconductor input was scheduled.” The semiconductor industry speculates that the $1.5 billion supply contract Groq signed with Saudi Arabia in February this year has been delayed. In December last year, Groq had entered into a contract with Saudi Aramco Digital to support data center construction. The $2 billion revenue forecast for this year was also a figure announced after the contract with Saudi Arabia. The Information reported, “After the contract with Groq, Saudi Arabia began importing AI chipsets from NVIDIA and AMD, and competition for data center capacity in the region intensified.” The outlook for chipset delivery next year also appears uncertain. Groq recently told investors that it expects revenue of $1.2 billion in 2026 and $1.9 billion in 2027. While this represents a steep growth from this year’s $500 million, it is a modest figure considering Groq’s self-proclaimed position as a competitor to NVIDIA and AMD in AI chipsets. For comparison, NVIDIA’s revenue for the first quarter (February-April) this year reached $44.06 billion. This is bad news for Samsung Electronics, which has positioned Groq as a key foundry partner.
