Title: Fed Rate Hike Calls Grow After US CPI Release - 'Need Compelling Reason to Hold Steady'

Published: Sat, 12 Sep 2026 20:05:00 GMT

(Seoul=Yonhap Infomax) Ji Eun Park - Following the August US Consumer Price Index (CPI) results that largely met market expectations, analysts are increasingly predicting the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates at next week's meeting. Experts assess that with inflation not slowing sufficiently and economic growth and employment remaining robust, it has become difficult to justify keeping rates on hold. According to the US Department of Labor on the 11th (local time), the August headline CPI rose 0.4% month-over-month and 3.4% year-over-year on a seasonally adjusted basis, matching market forecasts. Core CPI, excluding volatile food and energy prices, increased 0.3% month-over-month, slightly exceeding the market expectation of 0.2%, while rising 2.4% year-over-year, in line with market projections. "Economic growth remains very robust, the labor market is showing recovery, inflation has been above target for an extended period, and future inflation indicators suggest the upward trend will continue," said Matthew Luzzetti, chief US economist at Deutsche Bank. "The case for the Fed to raise rates next week is very strong." Previously, the US second-quarter real GDP revision showed a 1.5% annualized increase from the previous quarter on a seasonally adjusted basis, matching both the preliminary estimate and market expectations. Non-farm payrolls released on the 4th showed an increase of 162,000 jobs in August, significantly exceeding market expectations. Pooja Sriram, econ
