Oops, something went wrong SAN FRANCISCO, Sept. 10, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Embedder and SEGGER today announced a partnership that gives Embedder's AI agent direct access to SEGGER J-Link and J-Trace probes, enabling the firmware it writes to be tested and debugged on real hardware before it ships. Embedder is an AI coding agent built for embedded software engineering. Through J-Link, the agent interacts with the target hardware much like an engineer would: It flashes the build, sets breakpoints, inspects registers and memory, and monitors live output over RTT. When something fails, it continues debugging directly on the device until the code runs correctly. J-Link is the market-leading family of debug probes in the industry, supporting more than 15,000 devices across all major MCU families. Their high-speed programming and reliable target communication also make them well suited for autonomous agent demands. "AI agents are becoming much more than tools for generating code. They can analyze existing firmware, investigate unexpected behavior, identify bugs, and help develop and verify solutions," said Erik Loehr, Product Manager at SEGGER. "In embedded systems, however, this requires direct access to the real target hardware. By combining Embedder with SEGGER's professional debug and trace technology, we are enabling AI to participate in the entire embedded development cycle - from writing code to debugging and validating it on the device." "As AI agents become more capable, the biggest bottleneck in embedded development is shifting from writing firmware to verifying it meets functional and performance requirements on real hardware,," said Bob Wei, CTO and founder of Embedder. "By giving Embedder direct control of SEGGER's J-Link and J-Trace probes, we're enabling our agents to test, debug, and optimize the firmware they write.
